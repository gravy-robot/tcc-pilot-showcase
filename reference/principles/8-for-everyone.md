# Principle 8: Make it For Everyone

To engage all audiences in climate, focus on commonalities as opposed to differences. That means not giving the debate an unnecessary spotlight. Referencing denial arguments, even to shoot them down, can give the arguments an unnecessary spotlight when 99.9% of scientists agree on climate science and >70% of people globally want to see climate action. If it's necessary to mention climate misinformation, do so by preemptively warning that only a small % of people believe it, and only a small number of actors (e.g. the Carbon Majors) perpetuate it.

## Review guidance

**In play:** when the piece addresses climate disagreement or misinformation, uses political or identity
framing, or must work across differing views.

Check for unnecessary references to denial or debate, language that sorts readers into camps, assumptions of
shared politics, and missed common ground. A rebuttal can amplify the claim it repeats. If misinformation must
be addressed, establish its limited support before naming or repeating it. Do not add consensus or political
framing when the piece does not enter contested ground.
