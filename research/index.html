Messenger-centered design

Where does curated content support the messenger's journey? Nearly every stage. But to help, it has to show up in touchpoints TCC owns or in tools messengers already use every day.

A healthcare persona is the basis of the proposed journey below. The journey shows the role of example content at each stage.

Requirements for example content are translated into Jobs to be done. Each job links back to the stage, the persona line, and the research it comes from.

Jobs to be done #

Seven jobs, one per journey stage. Each shows where it comes from: the Journey stage, Dr. Maya's Persona voice, and the Research in TCC's and AU's landscape reports.

The core job

When I've decided climate belongs in my professional voice, I want to see someone like me doing it well, so I can do it myself without risking my credibility.

Being confident and being effective are separate outcomes. An example has to build both: belief that she can and should speak, and the skill to speak well.

Why examples, rather than training

  • In the TCC/AU research, communicators ranked regular updates on "what works" (54%) far above training (27%) and science education (24%).
  • The research also notes that findings rarely reach working professionals such as a busy doctor.
  • Dr. Maya's voice on the journey map: "Show me good examples. Don't lecture me."

A Content Example is any curated piece of real climate communication TCC shows a messenger: a single piece in the library; one half of a contrast pair (a strong piece and a weaker one); a reference the messenger's AI chatbot uses as a model; an attribution on the messenger's own post.

Research

  • The same survey table, unrounded: best-practice updates 53.5%, training 26.5%, science education 24.1%. (AU research report, PDF p. 17)

J5 · Stage 5: Create

Be a model she can borrow #

When I'm drafting a post, op-ed or handout, I want a proven model to borrow the structure and tone from, so my draft is fast, sounds like me and avoids jargon, doom and partisanship.
Practical

Emotional and social
"I don't want to sound like a doomer, or partisan."

Research

  • In our audit, both segments miss most often on P4 Doable and P8 For Everyone (partisan framing). (Example audit)

Research

Jobs for other stakeholders

StakeholderJob the Content Example does for them
TCCA unit of evidence about "what works," and something to amplify. In the audit, the strongest health videos had only 20 and 55 views, a distribution problem curation can fix.
Original creatorCredit, attribution and a wider audience for work they've already done.
Gatekeepers (hospital comms, medical societies)A precedent they can approve against. One approval can be reused across a health system.
AI tools (the messenger's chatbot)A structured, annotated reference that steers drafts toward the Eight Principles.

Underlying needs, across all stages

  1. Relevance: "Is this someone like me, about my patients, in my place?"
  2. Credibility: "Is it sourced, non-partisan and from a trusted institution?"
  3. Clarity about why it works: "What exactly makes this good?"
  4. Something reusable: "What can I take from it?"
  5. Safety: "Will this hurt my job or my patients' trust?"
  6. Connection: "Who else is doing this?"

Evidence caveats

  • The sample was different. The AU survey sampled professional climate communicators, not trusted messengers like clinicians. The "examples over training" finding fits Dr. Maya's persona, but it is borrowed evidence.
  • Dr. Maya is a composite. Her voice lines are illustrative, not interview quotes.
  • What to test first: the jobs at stages 4 to 6 (learn, create, share) carry the most design weight and the least direct evidence. Test them in clinician interviews before locking in requirements.
