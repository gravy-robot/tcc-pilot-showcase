Health professionals: from noticing climate in the clinic to sharing what works

How curated examples help a trusted health messenger at each stage of TCC's marketing funnel, training and enablement. The same seven stages will be used for the outdoor and athlete map.

Composite persona
Dr. Maya, family physician
Busy community practice. Sees heat and smoke in her older and asthmatic patients. Posts on LinkedIn a few times a month, drafts in an AI chatbot on her phone, and makes clinic handouts in Canva. Belongs to a medical society. Wary of anything that looks partisan.

GoalWhat she is trying to do
Understand why her patients do worse in heat and smoke, and whether doctors should say anything.
Find credible, practical guidance written for clinicians, not activists.
Get ongoing help without a big time commitment.
Learn what actually works, in minutes, not hours.
Draft a post, op-ed or patient handout quickly, in her own voice.
Publish without risk to her job or her patients' trust.
Know it made a difference and find peers doing the same.
DoingActions and channels
Sees a colleague's LinkedIn post, a medical society newsletter, or local news quoting a doctor on wildfire smoke.
Clicks through to talkingclimate.com. Tries the Start Talking tool. Searches for heat and older patients.
Signs up for the newsletter or a starter pack on her phone between appointments.
Takes a short online module, or a session at a society conference.
Opens an AI chatbot on her phone, Canva for a handout, and the LinkedIn composer.
Runs it past her employer's comms team. Posts on LinkedIn or YouTube, submits an op-ed, or hands it to patients.
Checks responses, joins TCC's network, and offers her piece for others to use.

Pain pointsWhat gets in the way
Climate content often reads as political or as activism. Little time to dig in.
Most resources speak to a general audience. Nothing filters by profession, topic or place.
Sign-ups ask for a lot and promise vague benefits like "join the network."
Communicators rank training low (27%) and evidence on what works high (54%).
AI drafts come back generic, jargon-heavy or doom-laden. No good examples in the tool.
Employer gatekeepers. Good health videos get little reach: 20 and 55 views in our audit.
Most communicators work alone and rarely hear what landed.
Curated content's jobExamples from the audit
Proof it's normal
Courtney Howard on wildfire smoke: a doctor, practical steps, no politics. Reshared by her society.
Hero example
Ashley Lawson's heat-dome speech leads a health library filtered by role, topic and place.
The offer
A starter pack of three good vs. so-so pairs, sent by email.
Contrast pairs
Lawson vs. the Seattle op-ed, Howard vs. Vossler. Grade it, then fix it.
In-tool references
Good examples load into her chatbot as models. A Canva handout follows Howard's structure.
Reassurance and reach
Examples clinicians have already published show comms teams what's safe. TCC reposts strong pieces.
Contribution loop
Her piece is graded and added to the library. A monthly "what's working" digest.
Opportunity for TCCWhat the platform could do
Place examples in partner channels: Medical Society Consortium, CAPE, Climate for Health.
A health landing page on talkingclimate.com with filters for role, topic and geography.
Make the conversion point "Get 3 patient-ready examples," not "Join our network."
15-minute modules built on contrast pairs. [Explore CME credit with a society partner.]
An assistant grounded in the curated library, such as Speak Sustainability, plus Canva templates.
"Submit for amplification" so TCC can repost and boost good work.
Peer cohorts by specialty, and a library that grows from members' own work.
