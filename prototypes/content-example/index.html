Content Example: compare three ways to present an example

The player that shows a curated example isn't decided yet. The requirements (section 7.2) name two candidate models, a Stage like a video site and a Canvas like a map app, plus a baseline built only from the requirements. All three use the same 18 audit examples, the same anatomy in the same order, and the same data. Only layout and how notes are revealed differ. We'll judge them on: can Dr. Maya tell in seconds whether it's for her, on a phone (J2)? Can she find why it works without being lectured (J4)? Can she see the pattern she'd borrow (J5)? Do long documents and video work equally well (J2, J4)? Does it export cleanly to a slide or PDF page (J4, J6)? And how much does it weigh on a slow connection (J1, J2; WSG 2.17, 3.1)?

Model A: Stage

Stage

A clear stage holds the artifact; details sit below on phones and beside it on desktop. Controls float over the stage's bottom edge. Tests: video, familiar reflow.

Canvas

The artifact fills the screen and pans and zooms. Details float in cards and a bottom sheet; pins open note cards. Tests: long documents, spatial annotation.

How to read these prototypes

  • Stand-ins, not copies. We don't have rights to reproduce the originals. Each one is drawn as an illustration of its format, filled with TCC's paraphrase and at most one short quote. Every stand-in links to the original.
  • Messenger and curator views. Messenger view hides So-so and Not-yet-usable examples except inside contrast pairs (rules R2, R3). Curator view shows all 18.
  • Draft curation. Pattern, step, topics, place and notes are derived from the audit for prototyping and labelled as drafts.
  • Gaps are shown. Where the audit has no data (full text, clinician review, institution, reshare counts), the page says so.
