The 2021 heat dome: a speech and an op-ed

Same event, same region. One patient story and one ask lands better than a list of disasters and ten asks.

The second piece is a learning case, not a callout of its authors (rule R4). Use this page as a draft precedent sheet: print it or save it as PDF.

Remembering the 2021 BC heat dome

Dr. Ashley Lawson

Why it works

Good example

She starts from what she saw working downtown Vancouver during the 2021 heat dome, so the story is personal and local. She moves from grief to a hopeful turn toward cheaper, cleaner energy, then asks for one thing anyone can do: check on isolated seniors.

What to watch

A few phrases could be plainer (P2 mixed), and it doesn't yet show others already acting (P6 mixed). It had 20 views in our audit: strong but unseen.

Extreme heat’s impact on our health demands we take action

Physicians · Washington Physicians for Social Responsibility (WA PSR)
  • Heat
  • Washington State, US
  • Evergreen · 2023

What works, and what to fix

So-so
  • P1+Personal: strong
  • P2~Accessible: mixed
  • P3~Empowering: mixed
  • P4~Doable: mixed
  • P5+Collective: strong
  • P6~Normal: mixed
  • P7+Trustworthy: strong
  • P8~For everyone: mixed

Some of the most vivid patient stories in the set, such as a man who had a heart attack waiting for roadside help on I-5.

What to watch (learning case)

It opens with a long list of disasters and despair, calls the science “no longer a matter of debate,” and ends with more than ten asks at once. Fix: lead with one patient, drop the debate, end with one ask.
